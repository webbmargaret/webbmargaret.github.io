A DATA REPORT FOR THE DISCIPLINE-BASED EDUCATION RESEARCH (DBER) COMMUNITY

Tracking the Postdoc Pipeline

Fifteen-plus years of job postings from two community-run boards — the PER Jobs blog and the Engineering Education job wiki that are scraped, classified, and read together.

By Dr. Margaret Webb (LinkedIn), Dr. Alexandra Coso Strong, and Alexandra Werth — Cornell University

A note on why this exists

Two boards, still mostly alone

PER Jobs and the EER wiki are the closest things physics and engineering education have to shared job boards — and they still barely overlap with each other. Most other DBER subfields — biology education, chemistry education, geoscience education, CS education, math education — don't have anything like either one. Postings circulate on listservs, X/Twitter threads, and word of mouth, which means people miss openings simply for being in the wrong subfield's social circle.

  • Know a job board for another Discipline-Based Education Research (DBER) subfield? Tell us — we'll add it to the pipeline and start tracking it here.
  • Don't have one for your field? The scraper and classifier are built to be forked — point them at a new URL and a new board is a few hours of work, not a few months.
  • Want to go further? A shared position-type taxonomy across fields — not a merged board, just a common vocabulary — would make it possible to aggregate postings without erasing any community's ownership of its own list.

§1 · Overview

Two blogs, one labor market

Since the mid-2000s, two volunteer-run pages have quietly served as the closest thing discipline-based education research (DBER) and engineering education research (EER) have to a shared job board: the PER Jobs blog, covering physics and science education research broadly, and the Engineering Education Community Resource wiki, focused on engineering education. Neither site was built for data analysis; they're primarily lists, updated by hand, aimed at people looking for their next position.

This report scrapes both, classifies every listing into a position type, and reads the two datasets side by side: what's being posted, how much of it is postdoctoral, how that's shifted since 2011 (EE) / 2007 (PER), and how much of that shift survives once you account for how much of both datasets is genuinely ambiguous or how thin some year-slices are.

Every chart below is interactive — hover for exact values, and where a number is an estimate rather than a count, there's a band or whisker showing how much to trust it.

This report grew out of a companion critical literature review, Building Capacity for Instructional Change: A Critical Review and Conceptual Framework for Discipline-Based Education Research (DBER) Postdoctoral Professional Development (Webb, Coso Strong, & Werth, presented at the 2026 ASEE Annual Conference & Exposition, Charlotte, NC, Paper #50607). That paper cites this same job-board data to motivate its central argument: growth in DBER postdoctoral positions doesn't by itself guarantee more capacity for instructional change in STEM classrooms — that capacity has to be built relationally, through the tensions and support structures postdocs navigate with faculty collaborators. The paper proposes a conceptual framework for how that happens; this page is the underlying data behind its motivating claims, extended and kept current. (Paper available on request until it's publicly indexed.)

§10 · How to Read the Uncertainty

Three different bands, three different meanings

This report uses uncertainty visualization in three different ways, deliberately not conflated into one generic "error bar," because they answer different questions:

WhereWhat it showsWhat it means
Fig. 2 hatched segmentsShare of listings classified Unknown / OtherClassifier coverage — how much of that bar's total is not confidently placed into any category
Fig. 4 shaded bandRolling standard deviation around the 5-yr meanVolatility — how much raw year-to-year counts vary within the smoothing window
Figs. 5 & 6 whiskers95% confidence interval for a proportionSampling uncertainty — how precisely a percentage is known given that year's total number of listings

None of these are error bars in the classical repeated-measurement sense — there was one scrape, not repeated trials. They're the closest statistically honest analogue available for observational count data like this.

§11 · Methodology

How this was built

1. Scraping

PER Jobs is a Blogspot blog, so the scraper walks its date-grouped post structure page by page, following the "Older Posts" link until it runs out, recording each post's title and the date header above it. The EER wiki is a PBworks page, edited in place rather than posted chronologically — so instead of one scrape, the EER scraper visits every saved revision of the page (over 500 of them) and extracts the job table from each, treating a job's first appearance across revisions as its posting date. This is slower and messier than a blog scrape, but it's the only way to recover history from a wiki that doesn't keep dated posts.

2. Date handling

PER Jobs dates come cleanly off the blog's own date headers. EER listings carry two dates: a free-text posting_date field typed by whoever edited the wiki (inconsistent formats, sometimes partial like "2018-11" or missing entirely — about 8% of rows) and a revision_date, the ISO date PBworks recorded when that row first appeared in a saved revision. This report uses revision_date throughout for EER, since it's complete and consistently formatted; it will occasionally lag the "true" posting date by however long a job sat on the wiki before a revision was saved.

3. Classification

Both datasets are classified with the same approach: an ordered list of regular-expression rules, checked top to bottom, first match wins. Postdoc and graduate-student patterns are checked before tenure-track/professor patterns specifically because titles like "Postdoc to TT Position" or mixed pipeline listings need the postdoc signal to win. PER's classifier carries extra patterns tuned to that board's heavy use of abbreviations (TT, NTT, VAP). Anything matching none of the rules falls into Unknown / Other — — of PER listings, — of EER listings.

§12 · Limitations

What this report doesn't do

  • No deduplication between boards. PER and EER were scraped and classified independently; a job cross-posted to both would count twice in any "combined" figure. Based on manual review at the time this project began, overlap between the two boards appeared to be small (a large majority of listings on each board don't appear on the other), but that estimate wasn't recomputed against the current data for this report.
  • 2026 is incomplete. Both scrapes were last run in early April 2026 (see §3), covering roughly the first quarter of that year. Anything involving 2026 — bars, points, or its contribution to the 2022–2026 rolling window in Fig. 4 — will fill in as the year continues; treat it as a floor, not a final count.
  • The classifier is rule-based, not learned. Regex keyword matching is transparent and auditable (you can read every rule in the source), but it will misfire on unusual phrasing, and "first match wins" ordering means edge cases sometimes land in a defensible-but-debatable category. The Unknown / Other share (§11) is the honest measure of how often it simply gives up.
  • Institution-level analysis is EE-only. PER Jobs titles are free text without a structured institution field, so Fig. 7 has no PER equivalent here. Extracting institutions from PER titles would need name-entity extraction beyond this pipeline's scope.
  • Both boards are volunteer-maintained and self-selecting. Neither is a comprehensive registry of every DBER/EER opening — they reflect what people chose to post, and posting norms (what gets listed, how promptly) may themselves have shifted over 15+ years in ways this data can't separate from real changes in the job market.

§13 · Code & Reproducibility

Scraper & classifier source

Full scripts are linked below and reproduced in part here. Everything here — scraping, classification, statistics, and this page — was built to be re-run: change the classification rules, re-scrape for more recent postings, or point the same pipeline at another job board.

perjobs_scraper_alljobs.py Blogspot pagination scraper — PER Jobs 
"""
PER Jobs Blog Scraper
=====================
Scrapes all job postings from the PER Jobs blog (perjobs.blogspot.com).
Extracts job titles and posting dates, saves to CSV, and prints summary statistics.

To scrape only a specific label (e.g. postdoc), set LABEL below.
Leave LABEL as None (or "") to scrape the full blog (all jobs).

Author(s): Dr. Margaret Webb authorizing and reviewing Claude Code
Date: April 2026
"""

import requests
from bs4 import BeautifulSoup
import pandas as pd
from datetime import datetime
import time
import re

# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Configuration
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

# Set to a label string (e.g. "postdoc") to filter, or None for ALL jobs.
LABEL = None

OUTPUT_FILENAME = "perjobs_listings.csv"

DELAY_SECONDS = 0.5


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Helpers
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def parse_date(date_string):
    """
    Convert "Tuesday, December 2, 2025" → "2025-12-02".
    Returns None if parsing fails.
    """
    try:
        date_without_day = re.sub(r'^\w+,\s*', '', date_string.strip())
        parsed_date = datetime.strptime(date_without_day, "%B %d, %Y")
        return parsed_date.strftime("%Y-%m-%d")
    except ValueError as e:
        print(f"Warning: Could not parse date '{date_string}': {e}")
        return None


def scrape_page(url, session):
    """
    Scrape a single blog page and return (jobs_list, next_page_url_or_None).
    Each job dict has keys: title, date.
    """
    jobs = []

    response = session.get(url)
    response.raise_for_status()

    soup = BeautifulSoup(response.text, 'html.parser')

    current_date = None

    main_content = soup.find('div', class_='blog-posts')
    if main_content:
        date_sections = main_content.find_all('div', class_='date-outer')

        for section in date_sections:
            date_header = section.find('h2', class_='date-header')
            if date_header:
                date_span = date_header.find('span')
                if date_span:
                    current_date = parse_date(date_span.get_text())

            post_titles = section.find_all('h3', class_='post-title')

            for title_element in post_titles:
                link = title_element.find('a')
                title = link.get_text(strip=True) if link else title_element.get_text(strip=True)

                if title and current_date:
                    jobs.append({'title': title, 'date': current_date})

    # Pagination: find the "Older Posts" link
    next_page_url = None
    older_link = soup.find('a', class_='blog-pager-older-link')
    if older_link and older_link.get('href'):
        next_page_url = older_link.get('href')

    return jobs, next_page_url


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Main scrape loop
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def scrape_all_jobs(label=LABEL, delay_seconds=DELAY_SECONDS):
    """
    Scrape all job postings from the PER Jobs blog.

    Args:
        label: If provided, scrape only that label (e.g. "postdoc").
               Pass None or "" to scrape the entire blog.
        delay_seconds: Polite wait between page requests.

    Returns:
        Pandas DataFrame with columns: title, date
    """
    if label:
        base_url = f"https://perjobs.blogspot.com/search/label/{label}"
        print(f"Scraping PER Jobs blog — label: '{label}'")
    else:
        base_url = "https://perjobs.blogspot.com/"
        print("Scraping PER Jobs blog — ALL jobs (no label filter)")

    session = requests.Session()
    session.headers.update({
        'User-Agent': 'PERJobsScraper/1.0 (Educational Research Project)'
    })

    all_jobs = []
    current_url = base_url
    page_count = 0

    print(f"Starting URL: {base_url}")
    print(f"Delay between requests: {delay_seconds}s")
    print("-" * 50)

    while current_url:
        page_count += 1
        print(f"Scraping page {page_count}: {current_url[:80]}...")

        try:
            jobs, next_url = scrape_page(current_url, session)
            all_jobs.extend(jobs)
            print(f"  Found {len(jobs)} jobs on this page (Total: {len(all_jobs)})")
            current_url = next_url
            if current_url:
                time.sleep(delay_seconds)

        except requests.RequestException as e:
            print(f"Error scraping page: {e}")
            break

    print("-" * 50)
    print(f"Scraping complete! Found {len(all_jobs)} total jobs.")

    df = pd.DataFrame(all_jobs)
    return df


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Summary
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def print_summary(df):
    """Print a summary of the scraped job data."""
    print("\n" + "=" * 60)
    print("SUMMARY OF SCRAPED JOB POSTINGS")
    print("=" * 60)
    print(f"\nTotal jobs found: {len(df)}")

    if len(df) > 0:
        df['_year'] = pd.to_datetime(df['date']).dt.year

        print("\nDistribution by year:")
        print("-" * 30)
        for year, count in df['_year'].value_counts().sort_index().items():
            print(f"  {year}: {count} jobs")

        print("\nSample entries:")
        print("-" * 30)
        for _, row in df.head(5).iterrows():
            print(f"  [{row['date']}] {row['title'][:70]}")

        df.drop('_year', axis=1, inplace=True)

    print("\n" + "=" * 60)


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Entry point
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

def main():
    df = scrape_all_jobs(label=LABEL, delay_seconds=DELAY_SECONDS)

    print_summary(df)

    df.to_csv(OUTPUT_FILENAME, index=False)
    print(f"\nData saved to: {OUTPUT_FILENAME}")
    print(df.to_string(max_rows=20))

    return df


if __name__ == "__main__":
    main()
classify_perjobs.py Ordered regex rules — excerpt (postdoc & grad-student rules shown in full; remaining rules follow the same pattern through Tenure-Track, Visiting, Lecturer, Staff, and Unknown/Other) 
"""
Position Type Classifier for PER Jobs Blog Listings
====================================================
Reads perjobs_listings.csv and adds a 'position_type' column
based on keyword/pattern matching in the 'title' field.

The PER Jobs blog uses heavy abbreviations (TT, VAP, NTT, FT, PER, DBER)
so patterns here are tuned specifically to that vocabulary.

Usage:
    python classify_perjobs.py
    python classify_perjobs.py --input perjobs_listings.csv --output perjobs_classified.csv
"""

import pandas as pd
import re
import argparse


# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
# Classification rules
# Order matters: rules are checked top-to-bottom and the FIRST match wins.
# Each rule is (label, list_of_regex_patterns).
# Patterns are matched case-insensitively against the full title string.
# ---------------------------------------------------------------------------

RULES = [
    # --- Postdocs / Research Fellows ---
    # Must come before TT/professor rules (some titles mix postdoc + TT pipeline)
    ("Postdoc / Research Fellow", [
        r"post[- ]?doc",
        r"postdoctoral",
        r"post doctoral",
        r"\bper postdoc\b",
        r"\bdber postdoc\b",
        r"stem.*postdoc",
        r"research fellow",         # "PER Research Fellow", "Teaching Research Fellow"
        r"gilbreth fellow",
        r"i-perf",
        r"hhmi.*fellow",
        r"zuckerman.*scholar",
        r"physics.*teaching.*fellow",   # e.g. "Physics Teaching Postdoctoral Fellow"
        r"teaching.*postdoctoral",
        r"science.*fellow.*per",
        r"per.*fellow",
        r"dber.*fellow",
    ]),

    # --- Graduate students / PhD positions / Studentships ---
    ("Graduate Student / PhD Fellowship", [
        r"\bphd student\b",
        r"\bphd position\b",
        r"doctoral fellow(?!ship)",    # "doctoral fellowship" → could be undergrad; "doctoral fellow" → grad
        r"doctoral position",
        r"graduate research assist",
        r"graduate assistantship",
        r"\bgrfp\b",
        r"nsf.*graduate.*fellow",
        r"studentship",               # common in UK/AU ads
        r"phd university assistant",
        r"per graduate",
        r"graduate.*per\b",
    ]),

    # ... additional rules continue below for Tenure-Track Faculty,
    # Visiting/Temporary, Lecturer, Professor of Practice, Staff, and
    # Unknown/Other — see the full source link below.
]

§14 · Reuse This Data

For the community

This page, its data, and its code are shared for other DBER/EER researchers, job-seekers, and program builders to use, check, and extend. If you find an error in the classification, a listing that should be reclassified, or want to propose a different "temporary/contingent" definition for §8, that's a legitimate scholarly disagreement — the rules are all above, in plain regex, specifically so they can be argued with.
