Teaching assistant and curriculum contributor for GRAD 5134, an interdisciplinary graduate course drawing students from engineering, social sciences, and humanities to address disaster resilience together. Supported as part of the [NSF Research Traineeship (NRT) program](https://www.nsf.gov/funding/pgm_summ.jsp?pims_id=505015){:target="_blank"} (Award #1735139). Responsible for grading weekly and project assignments with detailed feedback for 25 students, contributing to course design, syllabus development, and assessment instrument development, and collaborating with faculty on iterative course improvements.

## Curriculum and Assessment Contributions

Helped develop learning outcomes aligned with [Fink's Significant Learning Taxonomy](https://www.wiley.com/en-us/Creating+Significant+Learning+Experiences%3A+An+Integrated++Approach+to+Designing+College+Courses%2C+Revised+and+Updated+Edition-p-9781118124253){:target="_blank"}, targeting students' ability to:
- Identify weaknesses of disciplinary approaches
- Integrate methods across fields
- Analyze case studies at multiple scales
- Communicate research to diverse audiences
- Demonstrate collaborative skills across disciplines

Designed and analyzed **pre/post concept maps** tracking students' interdisciplinary conceptual understanding of disaster resilience (measured through diversity of nodes and density of cross-disciplinary connections). Analysis showed statistically significant improvement (p < 0.05) in students' understanding of disaster resilience from pre- to post-course assessment. Also conducted annual interviews exploring students' interdisciplinary development and contributed to program evaluation frameworks.

## Learning Design Highlights

Students worked in **interdisciplinary teams** to develop and evaluate community resilience strategies for real disaster case studies, grounding projects in realistic scenarios rather than abstract ones. This required navigating different methodological approaches and constraints toward integrated sociotechnical solutions — mirroring the realities of professional convergence research. Concept maps served triple duty: formative assessment of understanding, metacognitive exercises for externalizing thinking, and program evaluation data revealing curriculum effectiveness.

## NSF NRT Annual Meeting — Graduate Student Programming

Also planned and coordinated the graduate student track, *"Asking and Answering Convergent Research Questions,"* for the NSF NRT Annual Meeting (2022–2023). This multi-session program welcomed students from diverse disciplines, facilitated discussions on navigating institutional barriers to interdisciplinary work, and structured opportunities for students to practice formulating convergent research questions together — creating conditions for situated learning and future research collaboration.
