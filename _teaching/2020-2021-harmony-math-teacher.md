Designed and delivered lesson plans with active learning pedagogies for in-person and online geometry and pre-algebra courses aligned with [Texas Essential Knowledge and Skills (TEKS)](https://tea.texas.gov/academics/curriculum-standards/teks-review/texas-essential-knowledge-and-skills) standards. Built relationships with over 120 students, collaborated with faculty teaching teams to increase the use of active learning pedagogies across the math department, and contextualized subject matter to student interests to advance learning.

Earned **TExES 274 Teaching Certification** (Grades 6–12: Mathematics, Physical Science, and Engineering; score: 276/300).

## Pedagogical Approaches

My K-12 teaching foregrounded **backward design**: every activity — from Kahoot formative assessments to interdisciplinary projects — was explicitly anchored to specific TEKS learning outcomes. Key examples include:

**Kandinsky Geometry Project (High School):** Students created abstract art integrating trigonometric and circle theorem concepts, requiring them to move beyond procedural calculations toward deeper conceptual understanding sufficient to represent ideas visually and explain mathematical reasoning grounded in theorems. The required artist statement fostered reflective practice and interdisciplinary boundary-crossing between math and art.

**Real-World Geometry Hunt (7th Grade):** Students engaged with authentic, place-based problems — measuring objects in their homes and communities — practicing technical communication through presentations and written reflections while developing metacognitive awareness about decomposition strategies.

**Composite Volume Unit (Pre-Algebra):** Built understanding through scaffolded conceptual progression (2D composite areas → 3D composite volumes), helping students construct knowledge through structured sequencing rather than isolated sub-units. Exit ticket data informed instruction day-to-day, creating responsive feedback loops.
