Supported Rice University's mechanical engineering ABET reaccreditation process and undergraduate laboratory curriculum revision as an advisory board representative. Applied systems thinking to audit course learning outcomes, identify misalignments between stated goals and instructional activities, and redesign laboratory experiences to foreground authentic engineering competencies.

## Curriculum Audit and Revision

Working with faculty, audited existing undergraduate labs against Rice's stated learning outcomes and [ABET Engineering Accreditation Criteria](https://www.abet.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2025-2026_EAC_Criteria.pdf){:target="_blank"}, removing misaligned tasks and ensuring effort across the curriculum advanced core goals. Key contributions:

**Strain Gauge Lab Redesign** — The original lab required students to spend the majority of lab time writing code, which was not an explicit learning outcome and displaced learning about how strain gauges function under tension toward designing simple devices. Revised the lab to provide pre-written code so students could focus on interpreting gauge responses, predicting how gauges might react to various configurations, and applying understanding to prototype simple bending beam sensor designs. The redesign made labs more active, better aligned with relevant learning outcomes, and immediately applicable — with coding skills addressed in other labs where it was explicitly an outcome.

**Student Surveys for Continuous Improvement** — Implemented student surveys to serve as ongoing improvement feedback loops aligned with organizational systems practices, treating assessment as a responsive mechanism rather than a terminal event.

**Undergraduate Advisory Board Participation** — Presented updated curriculum and learning outcomes to students to identify overlaps between courses and perceived gaps in preparation, and gathered student feedback on courses they would like to see added based on an overview of ABET requirements — applying systems thinking to educational contexts by sharing curriculum-level understanding with peers.
