DBER postdoctoral positions have grown alongside the field, yet little scholarship examines how postdocs actually develop capacity for instructional change — or what supports that development. This poster presents two connected papers advancing a research agenda on DBER postdoc professional development.

The first paper (ASEE 2026, full paper) reports a theory-focused critical literature review synthesizing STEM postdoc professional development scholarship and DBER faculty professional development research through the lens of relational agency. Rather than treating the challenges postdocs face as problems to eliminate, the review reframes them as "critical tensions" — productive mismatches between role demands and preparation that, when navigated through relationships, can build capacity for instructional change. We present four propositions and a conceptual model describing how DBER postdoc agency emerges relationally: through interactions involving delegation, self-agentification, and recognition or rejection between established change agents and agents-in-the-making, supported by "relational infrastructures" like communities of practice, productive feedback loops, and co-constructional mentorship.

The second paper (in progress) takes this framework to data. Drawing on semi-structured interviews with four faculty and five postdoctoral scholars at a single R1 institution, we apply the relational agency model to two faculty-postdoc dyads in engineering departments. To address the identifiability of relational data, we developed a composite research fiction vignette — the story of a doctoral scholar, Morgan Osei — that preserves the framework-relevant dynamics while protecting participants. We present our structural and axial coding approach, the thematic trajectories we identified across dyads, and what the data suggest about conditions under which critical tensions lead to engagement versus disengagement.

Together, these papers move the field from conceptualizing postdoc agency toward observing and supporting it. We invite conversation about the framework, the methodology, and implications for designing postdoc positions and professional development programs that treat relational dynamics as central — not incidental — to building DBER's capacity for instructional change.
