Responding to how fragmented information architectures perpetuate communication
barriers and hinder service delivery and cross-sector collaboration, we identified a
need for a reimagined and centralized database to map services for newcomer refugee
populations in the state of Virginia. Through a multi-institutional research consortium
with resettlement agencies, migrant advocates, and education institutions, we secured
funding to develop a "Resettlement Services for New Americans in Virginia" database
and accompanying website that reimagines how information about refugee services is
produced, organized, and accessed by centering community voices and knowledge in
design. By collating disparate resources into one database and iterating based on
community feedback, our initiative supports not just service delivery and stakeholder
collaboration, but refugee integration outcomes.
In this paper, we describe our project and showcase how digital tools can transform
communication across nonprofit, government, and grassroots organizations while
centering community voices in design processes. Our work investigates how
centralized digital infrastructure can reshape rhetorical and digital practices in service
ecosystems and enhance collaborative knowledge sharing across boundaries.
