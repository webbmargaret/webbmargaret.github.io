In this article, we present our community-centered and collaborative work where we developed a digital information and communication infrastructure through a centralized database of Virginia service providers offering support to newcomers. Our project, supported by institutional and external funding, brings together an interdisciplinary team of researchers from multiple institutions across Virginia to reimagine digital justice and enhance access to vital resources for new Americans. Responding to community and service organization needs, and using participatory design methods, we investigated how professional communicators can better facilitate information flows and stakeholder coordination in complex service environments. By consolidating dispersed information into a single, user-friendly and integrated professional communication platform, our initiative aims to enhance organizational knowledge sharing and cross-sector information flows and bridge the gap between service providers and those in need. This essay describes our theoretical grounding in developing digital information technologies with communities, the processes by which we developed the technologies, as well as our insights and plans for future development and user testing.
