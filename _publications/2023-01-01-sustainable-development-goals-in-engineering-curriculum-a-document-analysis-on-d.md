Higher education’s contribution to sustainable development (SD) has become
more evident to researchers and engineers in the last two decades, spurring interest
in engineering education for sustainable development (EESD). While numerous ways of integrating SD into engineeringcurricula, programming, and mission statements exist, the level, depth, and type of
integration vary across institutions worldwide, and existing research is limited in
its understanding of how EESD is effectively implemented. With this in mind, and
considering previous research on SD in higher education that suggests institutional
strategies, curricula, and research goals both reflect and forecast the values and
impact institutions have on sustainability more broadly (Bolman & Deal, 2008;
Ramirez-Mendoza et al., 2020; Roth & Elrod, 2015), it is important to unpack the
ways institutions and engineering departments frame EESD as part of their
commitments. This qualitative exploratory study provides an examination of how universities
verbalize and initiate their priorities, values, and strategies towards SD in higher
education, specifically looking at top-ranking universities in the 2021 Times
Higher Education Impact Ranking for their performance towards achieving the
United Nations’ Sustainable Development Goals (UN SDGs). Using a
comparative document analysis approach, these successful public institutions’
documents were analyzed for their commitments and initiatives towards SD as
well as comparisons across institutions and the disciplinary departments within
them. Our findings indicate that university initiatives toward SD appear across
university areas including strategic plans, research, curriculum, and co-curricular
activities. That said, there are variations and conflicting descriptions of
“sustainability” and “sustainable development” across these documents, even
within individual institutions and departments. It is critical for institutions to
organize themselves meaningfully around commitments towards SD and as
“living laboratories for sustainable development” (Ramirez-Mendoza et al., 2020),
though it is challenging to do so across diverse contexts and institutions without a better understanding of what organizational structures, vocabularies, or local
politics impinge upon the implementation of SDGs in curricula.
While this study looks exclusively at documents available on university websites
and may not present a complete picture of the university initiatives, this analysis
enhances our understanding of how universities prioritize SD in their vision,
priorities, and branding and the ways in which organizational structures interact
with these efforts. The work expands upon previous scholarship, noting barriers to
implementing SD initiatives in higher education and motivating future study to
improve theories for expressing commitments towards SD across organizations,
perform analysis of how wording and terminologies vary across contexts, and
learn how to bridge disciplinary silos towards positive university-level SDG
impact.
